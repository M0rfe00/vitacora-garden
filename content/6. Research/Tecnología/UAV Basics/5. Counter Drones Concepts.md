# Mod 5: Drone Defenders
___
Students of this program are likely to be tasked as a whitehat hackers or defenders of assets against drone threats

Commercial needs to defend both ground and airborne assets from drone threats are increasing

Legal considerations are just as important as technical concerns

Rapid innovation creates a drone arms race of attacker and defender technology

# Planning Drone Defense Operations
6 step process to cover the basics of establishing a counter drone enforcement area
1. Identify critical asset or target
2. Define success - destroy - deny-capture - evade - time
3. Identify available tools and methods
4. List likely or known threats
5. Select tools that meet threat
6. Deploy tools and execute plan

# Step 1: Identify critical asset
What am I defending? Define it in physical terms

Is it fixed or mobile - building or vehicles

Small or large - Single person or entire airport

Sector or bubble - linear border of a country or airspace around an entire airport


# STEP 2: DEFINE SUCCESS
- How much time is allowed to detect, identify, intercept and either destroy or impact the capability of the threat?
- Do I need to destroy every flying thing that comes near me?
- Do I need to spy on what is spying on me to learn more about it?
- Can I simply hide from the drone and keep doing my job?
- How accurate must my alarms be?
- Do I just need to do this once or repeat very quickly(swarm)
- Who do I report any threat events to and what is required?

# Step 3: Tools and Methods
- Identify legal considerations around methods FIRST!
	- As a commercial or civil entity can I destroy a hobby drone that flies by the airport in a threatening manner? Who is liable and what authority directs this action?
	- If I am allowed to destroy it, what method is approved? Technical intercept, kinetic impact, bird recovery?
	- Local ordinance and national laws will both apply
- More than one governing body may apply rules – FAA and FCC
- Allowed methods will then dictate what tools you might choose from
## Methods continued
Drone defense is not just blowing up flying things

A variety of degrees of intercept exist :
- Kinetic strike - blow it up
- Identify and observe - can give clues on capability
- Intercept control or payload signals - spy on the spies
- Force down for capture
- Capture with other flying things - birds / larger drones
- Spoof or deceive - show them what they want to see...

# Step 3: Tools
Tools are usually the fun part
Variety of sizes and functions
Usually designed for one kind of defense (kinetic or not)
Often orchestrated into an ecosystem of defense

RF Weapons
- Used to detect platforms with RADAR and used to jam frequency ranges
- Small enough for 1 person or as large as a truck or multiple truck locations

Directed Energy - LASER
- Can be used to detect and identify
- Can be used to destroy
- Can be used to disrupt onboard sensors without destruction (optical jamming)


Interceptors
- Hawks have been trained to catch drones and down or even retrieve them
- Kinetic Drones that simply fly and crash into the target drone
- Nets, shotguns, foam, metal chaff and flares have all been deployed by interceptor drones to down a target drone midflight
- Safety concerns where will the drone and any pieces land?

Remote Hacking
- As drones incorporate more computing power, they increase vulnerability risk to hacking
- Custom or propriety signaling, encoding and encryption all leave room for undisclosed zero day threats
- Functionality is primary over security in current drone designs - hackers have room to work
- Sniffing the video stream can be just as useful as destroying the drone

# STEP 4: IDENTIFY THREATS
___
- News reports of recent intrusive behavior or attacks
- Observing your immediate environment - friend or foe in crowded airspaces?
- Known activities of adversary or what tools they have on hand
- Identify threat actors and their desired outcome, steal information or harm property or people

# STEP 5: SELECT TOOLS
___
- Use info from previous steps to decide what tool will work best
- Factors such as mobile or fixed asset may cut your choices of tools in half
- Time to respond and desired outcome will be another major deciding factor
- Cost and who is paying always matters - Am I a defender for hire with my own equipment or am I helping build a large defense posture for an airport?
- Your final list of choices will likely be short and specific to your need

# STEP 6: GET TO WORK - STRATEGY
- Deploy your system and execute test scenarios if possible
- Practice an event end to end often - check every stage for performance - time to detect, accuracy of identification, time to respond or intercept, reporting both machine and human driven
- Identify fallback procedure if long distance intercept doesn't work, what is plan B,C?
- How quickly can you reset for another response?
